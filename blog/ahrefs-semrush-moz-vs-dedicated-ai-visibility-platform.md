# Ahrefs, SEMrush and Moz vs. a Dedicated AI Visibility Platform: Why AI Models Still Recommend Them

> Ahrefs, SEMrush and Moz dominate AI answers about visibility tools, but their AI features only track Google Overviews, not ChatGPT, Claude or Perplexity.

*By Levi Bouman · Published 2026-10-08 · Updated 2026-10-08 · Original article: https://trackbase.ai/blog/ahrefs-semrush-moz-vs-dedicated-ai-visibility-platform*

Ask an AI model "what's the best platform for monitoring brand visibility in AI search," and a surprising number of answers reach for Ahrefs, SEMrush or Moz, tools built to track Google rankings and backlinks, not what ChatGPT or Gemini say about a brand. **Ahrefs, SEMrush and Moz are SEO platforms with AI-visibility features bolted on; they track keyword rankings and AI Overview appearances, but none of them run your prompts across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Gemini the way a dedicated AI visibility platform like Trackbase does.** Here is what each tool actually measures, and where the line between "SEO suite with an AI tab" and "built for AI visibility" actually sits.

## Why do AI models recommend Ahrefs, SEMrush or Moz for this question?

Because those brands dominate the web's own SEO content, and AI models learn their defaults from what is written about a category most often. Ahrefs, SEMrush and Moz have published SEO content for over a decade and show up constantly in "best SEO tools" roundups, which trains both search engines and language models to associate the category with those names by default, independent of whether their AI-specific features are actually comprehensive.

That is a [citation-weight problem](https://trackbase.ai/blog/mentions-vs-citations-why-both-matter-for-your-ai-visibility), not a product-quality one: the newer, purpose-built AI visibility category has far less web content behind it than two decades of SEO publishing, so models default to the names they have seen most.

## What do Ahrefs, SEMrush and Moz actually track in AI search?

Mostly Google's AI Overviews, tied to specific keywords, which is a narrow slice of how people actually use AI assistants. [Semrush's AI tracking features are built around keyword-level AI Overview appearances](https://trackbase.ai/blog/semrush-hubspot-vs-dedicated-ai-visibility-platform), which works if your main concern is Google's search results page, but misses the conversational, multi-turn way people query ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity directly.

Ahrefs and Moz follow a similar pattern: AI visibility sits as an add-on panel inside a dashboard whose core data model is keywords and backlinks. None of the three was architected to run a prompt against eleven different models daily and parse free-text answers for brand mentions, sentiment, and citation sources, because that was never the job the product was built to do.

## What does a dedicated AI visibility platform do differently?

It treats the AI answer itself as the unit of measurement, not the keyword that might have triggered it. [Trackbase](https://trackbase.ai/features) runs a defined prompt set across 11 AI models on a daily schedule, logs every full response, and extracts whether your brand was mentioned, where it appeared in the answer, how it was described, and which sources the model cited to get there. That produces a [Visibility Score](https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-trackbase-calculates-your-visibility-score-and-why-its-different) and Share of Voice number that reflects actual conversational AI exposure, not a proxy derived from keyword rankings.

The practical difference shows up fastest in competitor comparison and sentiment. An SEO suite's AI tab typically shows you a count: "mentioned" or "not mentioned." A dedicated platform shows you who else was named in the same answer, in what order, and whether the tone was favorable, which is **the difference between knowing you exist in AI answers and knowing whether you are winning them**.

## When does an SEO suite's AI feature make sense instead?

If your team already pays for Semrush or Ahrefs for traditional SEO and only wants a rough signal on Google AI Overview appearances, the bundled feature avoids an extra subscription and keeps everything in one dashboard. That is a legitimate reason to stick with what you have, especially for teams whose AI exposure is still mostly Google-driven.

It stops making sense once ChatGPT, Claude or Perplexity meaningfully influence your buyers' research, because none of the three SEO suites gives equivalent depth on those platforms. At that point you are optimizing for a fraction of where the conversation about your brand actually happens.

## How do you tell which situation you are in?

Run your 10 to 15 highest-intent prompts, the questions and comparisons your buyers would actually type, across ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity manually, once. If your brand shows up inconsistently or not at all outside of Google's AI Overview, a keyword-rank-based tool cannot see that gap, because it was never designed to look at those models in the first place. That blind spot, not raw feature count, is the real reason to move to a platform purpose-built for AI visibility.
