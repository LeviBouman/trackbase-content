# Being Mentioned by AI Isn’t Enough: Why Sentiment Decides Who Wins

> Two brands appear in the same ChatGPT answer. One is described as a solid, well-reviewed option that teams trust. The other is listed as an option, though the model adds that some users report slow support. Both were mentioned. Only one is getting the click.

*By Bodrik Bakker · Published 2026-06-26 · Updated 2026-11-13 · Original article: https://trackbase.ai/blog/being-mentioned-by-ai-isnt-enough-why-sentiment-decides-who-wins*

A mention in an AI answer is only worth something if the way the model describes you makes a buyer keep reading. Two brands can appear in the same ChatGPT answer: one as a solid, well-reviewed option teams trust, the other as an option the model adds "some users report slow support" to. Both were mentioned, but only one is getting the click. Visibility tells you that you showed up; sentiment tells you whether showing up helped.

## Presence is not the same as endorsement

It is easy to treat a mention as a win: you searched, your brand was there, job done. But a mention is just the door. How the model talks about you decides whether the buyer walks through it. This is a different axis from whether you are [mentioned or cited](https://trackbase.ai/blog/mentions-vs-citations-why-both-matter-for-your-ai-visibility). You can be both and still lose, if the description plants doubt.

## Where AI sentiment comes from

Models do not invent an opinion of you. They aggregate the language already out there: reviews, forum threads, press coverage, comparison pages and your own copy. That tone is distilled into how you are described. If your G2 reviews keep raising the same complaint, that complaint tends to leak into the answer. The mechanics are the same signals that drive [which brands AI recommends](https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-ai-models-decide-which-brands-to-recommend) in the first place.

In short, AI sentiment is a mirror of how the internet talks about you, filtered through the model.

## The three states, and what each one does

| State | What the answer sounds like | Effect on the buyer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Positive | "A well-reviewed option many teams trust" | Reinforces the recommendation and often lifts you higher in the answer |
| Neutral | "One of several options" | You are on the list, but nothing tips the buyer toward you over the next name |
| Negative or hedged | "Some users report slow support" | The mention carries a warning, sometimes worse than being absent |

This is why mention count alone is a misleading metric. Ten neutral mentions can be worth less than three warm ones.

## What sentiment looks like when you actually measure it

Sentiment is not one number for the whole market; it differs per model. In Trackbase's own tracked prompts over the last 7 days (data pulled on 13 November 2026), the average citation sentiment score was 72 out of 100, with 35 positive, 11 neutral and no negative citations out of 46. Per model, the sentiment score was 79 in ChatGPT, 74 in Gemini and 68 in Perplexity and Google AI Mode. Same brand, same week, three different tones, because the models lean on different sources.

That spread is the point: a single headline score hides the model where you sound lukewarm. If you only look at the average, you will not know which platform needs work.

## How to read and improve it

Treat sentiment as something you track, not a vibe you guess at.

1.  **Measure it per platform and per prompt.** You can read as trusted in ChatGPT and lukewarm in [Perplexity](https://trackbase.ai/blog/the-11-ai-platforms-every-brand-should-be-tracking-in-2026), simply because they lean on different sources.

2.  **Find the phrases that repeat.** Read the actual answers for hedging words such as "however", "some users", "limited" or "expensive", and note which source the model cites next to them.

3.  **Fix the sources, not the symptom.** Address the recurring review complaint, refresh the outdated comparison page, and earn balanced coverage that gives the model better language to draw on.

4.  **Answer the objection on your own site.** If the hedge is "slow support", publish your support hours, response targets and escalation path in plain language where a crawler can read it.

5.  **Re-check after changes.** Sentiment moves as your sources move, so what you fixed in March can read differently by summer.

## A simple sentiment audit for commercial teams

Run this once a month and keep the results in one sheet:

-   Pick 10 prompts a buyer would ask, including 3 direct comparisons with a named competitor.

-   Record for each prompt and model: mentioned yes/no, position in the answer, tone (positive, neutral, hedged) and the sources cited.

-   Highlight every hedged answer and trace it to the source behind it.

-   Assign an owner per source: customer success for reviews, PR for press, marketing for your own pages.

-   Re-run the same prompts four weeks later and compare.

## FAQ

### Can a negative AI mention be worse than no mention?

Yes. An absent brand leaves the buyer neutral, while a hedged mention gives them a concrete reason to skip you. That is why sentiment should be read next to presence, not after it.

### How do I improve how AI models describe my brand?

Change what the models read. Resolve recurring review complaints, update outdated comparison pages, earn balanced third-party coverage and state your answers to common objections clearly on your own site. Then re-measure, because sentiment follows the sources.

### Is sentiment the same in every AI model?

No. Each model weights different sources, so the same brand can sound trusted in one and lukewarm in another. Track sentiment per model and per prompt rather than relying on one blended score.
