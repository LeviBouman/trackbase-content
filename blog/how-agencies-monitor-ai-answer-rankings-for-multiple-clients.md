# How Agencies Monitor AI Answer Rankings for Multiple Clients

> What agencies need to track AI answer rankings for many clients: workspaces, daily prompt runs, stored responses and white-label reports, with a 30-minute setup.

*By Bodrik Bakker · Published 2026-11-25 · Updated 2026-11-25 · Original article: https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-agencies-monitor-ai-answer-rankings-for-multiple-clients*

## Short answer

Agencies monitoring AI answers for several clients need four things: a separate workspace per client, a fixed [prompt](https://trackbase.ai/glossary/prompt) set per client that runs every day, stored AI responses they can quote, and reporting that can be branded and sent without manual work. Tools built for one brand break down at around five clients because prompt limits, exports and logins multiply. Pick a platform that treats the client as the unit, not the seat.

## Why one-brand tools fail at agency scale

Most [AI visibility](https://trackbase.ai/glossary/ai-visibility) tools were designed for a marketer tracking their own brand. An agency has a different problem: ten or thirty brands, each with its own competitors, language and reporting rhythm. Three things go wrong first.

-   **Prompt limits multiply.** If a plan includes 20 prompts for one brand, 15 clients means 15 plans or a custom deal.

-   **Data gets mixed.** Competitor lists and prompt sets that overlap between clients make reports unreliable, and one wrong share-of-voice number in a client meeting costs trust.

-   **Reporting stays manual.** If every monthly report starts with screenshots, the margin on your [GEO](https://trackbase.ai/glossary/generative-engine-optimization) retainer disappears.

AgencyAnalytics makes a similar point in its [guide to LLM rank tracking tools](https://agencyanalytics.com/blog/llm-rank-tracking-tools): AI tracking is priced very differently across tools, and costs climb quickly once you track a whole client roster instead of one brand.

## The five requirements for multi-client monitoring

| Requirement | What to check | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client workspaces | Separate prompts, competitors and users per client | No data leaks between clients |
| Daily prompt runs | Same prompts, same models, every day | You can show trends, not single snapshots |
| Stored responses and citations | The full AI answer and its sources | You quote what the AI said and see which pages it trusted |
| Model coverage | ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI Overview and AI Mode, plus Claude and Copilot where clients need it | Different clients are found in different models |
| [White-label reporting](https://trackbase.ai/glossary/white-label-reporting) | Your logo, your domain, scheduled delivery | The deliverable looks like yours |

The AgencyAnalytics guide puts the same emphasis on prompt and response capture: a visibility percentage tells you something changed, but the stored response tells you what the AI actually said.

## How to set up a client in 30 minutes

1.  **Create the workspace** and add the client's brand, domain and the three to five competitors the client actually loses deals to.

2.  **Write 10 to 20 prompts** in the language the client's buyers use. For Dutch clients, run the prompts in Dutch, not translated English; see our guide on [tracking brand sentiment in Dutch AI answers](https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-to-track-brand-sentiment-in-dutch-ai-answers).

3.  **Group the prompts by funnel stage:** category discovery, comparison, alternatives and brand-specific questions.

4.  **Record a baseline.** Run for a week before you report anything, because a single day of AI answers is noisy.

5.  **Set the reporting cadence.** Monthly white-label report for the client, weekly internal review of what changed.

## What to report per client

Keep the report short enough that a client reads it. Four numbers cover most conversations:

-   **Visibility Score:** how often and how prominently the brand appears across the tracked prompts.

-   **Share of Voice:** the brand's share of all brand mentions against the competitor set. Our post on [how to measure AI share of voice](https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-to-measure-ai-share-of-voice) explains the calculation.

-   **Citations:** which URLs the models cite, and whether those are the client's pages or third parties.

-   **Sentiment:** whether mentions are positive, neutral or negative.

Then add one page of actions: the two or three content or technical fixes with the largest expected effect. That page is what turns monitoring into a retainer.

## FAQ

### How many prompts should an agency track per client?

Ten to twenty prompts per client is enough for a baseline: a few category discovery prompts, a few comparison and alternatives prompts, and brand-specific questions. Add more only when a client has several product lines or markets.

### Can I run the same prompts for every client?

Not as a rule. Shared prompts make reports look identical and hide what is specific to each client. Use a common template for structure, but write the prompts around each client's customers, competitors and language.

### How often should I send reports to clients?

Monthly for the client, weekly for your own team. AI answers shift from day to day, so daily data with a monthly summary gives the client a stable trend instead of a noisy chart.
