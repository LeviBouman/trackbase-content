# How to Improve Your AI Visibility: A Practical Playbook

> You already know AI visibility matters. The harder question is what to actually do about it on Monday morning. This is the owned-side playbook: the work on your own properties that you fully control. For the off-site half, see the guide on earning AI citations.

*By Levi Bouman · Published 2026-07-02 · Updated 2026-11-23 · Original article: https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-to-improve-your-ai-visibility-a-practical-playbook*

To improve your AI visibility, start with the buyer prompts you want to win, make the pages that answer them easy to retrieve and quote, add the content formats models cite most, and measure the result daily across models. Owned content is only half the job: models also weigh independent mentions, so pair this playbook with an off-site citation plan.

This is the owned-side playbook: the work on your own properties that you fully control. For the off-site half, see the guide on [earning AI citations](https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-to-get-cited-by-ai-earning-mentions-on-the-sources-models-trust).

## Step 1: Start with the prompts that matter

You cannot optimise for everything, so do not try. Write down the real questions buyers ask AI in your category and group them:

-   **Category questions**: "What is the best tool for X?"

-   **Comparison questions**: "X vs Y" and "alternatives to Y".

-   **Use-case questions**: "How do I do Z with limited budget?"

That list is your target. Everything below points at it. In [prompt tracking](https://trackbase.ai/features/prompt-tracking) you can follow the same list daily, so you always know which prompts you win and which you lose.

## Step 2: Structure pages so models can read them

Retrieval favours pages that are easy to extract from. Vague intros and walls of text get passed over for cleaner sources. Write for extraction:

-   Use clear H2s phrased as the question, then answer it in the first sentence underneath.

-   Lead with the claim, then support it. Self-contained statements are what models lift into answers.

-   Keep paragraphs short and scannable. Dense blocks bury the point.

-   Add an FAQ section, mark it up with FAQPage schema, and use Schema.org [structured data](https://trackbase.ai/features/structured-data) so machines can parse the page.

-   Make sure [AI crawlers](https://trackbase.ai/features/crawler-analytics) can actually reach the page: no accidental robots.txt blocks, no content hidden behind scripts.

A note on llms.txt: it is an emerging convention that some AI crawlers may read, but Google has said its Search does not use it. Treat it as optional and harmless, not as a lever. Google's own guidance on generative search stresses indexable, genuinely useful content over special files; we break it down in [what Google's AI optimization guide means for GEO](https://trackbase.ai/blog/googles-ai-optimization-guide-what-it-means-for-geo).

## Step 3: Build the content AI reaches for

Some formats earn their way into [AI answers](https://trackbase.ai/blog/monitor-changes-in-ai-answers-over-time) far more often than others. Prioritise them:

| Format | Why models cite it | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Honest comparison pages | They answer "X vs Y" prompts directly | You against the alternatives buyers weigh |
| Use-case pages | They mirror how buyers phrase needs | "AI visibility for Dutch e-commerce" |
| Definitive answers | They give a quotable first sentence | "What is share of voice in AI search?" |
| Data-led posts | They contain facts a model can attribute | Your own benchmark with a stated method |

Avoid commodity content. Pages that restate what ten other pages say give a model no reason to pick yours. First-hand data, real examples and a clear opinion do.

## Step 4: Strengthen the signals around your brand

Owned content gets you part of the way. But models weight independent mentions more heavily than anything you publish about yourself, which is why [what AI models already know about you](https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-ai-models-decide-which-brands-to-recommend) comes largely from third parties. Earning those [citations](https://trackbase.ai/blog/mentions-vs-citations-why-both-matter-for-your-ai-visibility) is its own playbook, covered in the [guide to getting cited by AI](https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-to-get-cited-by-ai-earning-mentions-on-the-sources-models-trust). Do not skip it: owned and earned work together.

Also check how you are described, not just whether you appear. A mention with the wrong pricing or a lukewarm tone costs you the click; see [why sentiment decides who wins](https://trackbase.ai/blog/being-mentioned-by-ai-isnt-enough-why-sentiment-decides-who-wins).

## Step 5: Measure, then iterate

AI optimisation is a loop, not a launch. Models update constantly, so treat your changes as experiments:

1.  Baseline where you appear across platforms before you change anything.

2.  Change one thing at a time so you can attribute movement.

3.  Re-measure after a week or two and keep what works.

4.  Review which URLs models cite for your prompts and fill the gaps first.

Without that loop you are optimising in the dark. [Trackbase](https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-trackbase-calculates-your-visibility-score-and-why-its-different) gives you the baseline and the day-by-day movement across 11 AI models, so the playbook becomes a measurable cycle instead of a guess. For a prioritised order of work, read [how to prioritize content and technical fixes](https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-to-prioritize-content-and-technical-fixes-for-ai-recommendations). None of this is a quick win. All of it compounds.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to improve AI visibility?

Expect weeks, not days. Retrieval-based answers can change within a week or two of publishing a stronger page, while changes to what a model "knows" from training take much longer. Measure weekly and judge trends rather than single days.

### Do I need new content or can I fix existing pages?

Start with existing pages that already rank or get cited: restructure them with question headings and direct answers. Add new pages where your prompt list shows a gap, such as comparisons or use cases you do not cover.

### How do I know if the changes worked?

Track a fixed set of prompts before and after, and compare presence rate, share of voice and cited sources per model. The [free AI visibility checker](https://trackbase.ai/free-ai-visibility-checker) gives you a first baseline in minutes.
