# What Software Can Help Me Track Rankings in AI Search Engines?

> Semrush and Ahrefs were not built to watch what ChatGPT says about you. Here is what AI rank tracking software actually needs: multi-model coverage, citation tracking and daily refresh.

*By Levi Bouman · Published 2026-10-05 · Original article: https://trackbase.ai/blog/best-software-to-track-rankings-in-ai-search-engines*

Most teams trying to track their brand in AI answers reach for the tool everyone already pays for: Google Analytics, Semrush, or a keyword rank tracker. None of them were built to watch what ChatGPT says about you, and bending them to that purpose produces gaps you will not notice until a client asks why a competitor outranks you in an AI answer you never checked. **Tracking your brand in AI search engines requires dedicated software built for prompt-based monitoring across multiple models, because generic SEO and analytics tools cannot see inside an AI-generated answer.**

The market for this category is new and moving fast, which makes the buying decision confusing. Here is what the software actually needs to do, and how to tell real AI visibility tracking from a repackaged SEO dashboard.

## What does AI search rank tracking software actually need to do?

It needs to run a defined set of prompts across multiple AI models on a recurring schedule and report whether, how, and where your brand appears in each response. That is a fundamentally different job than traditional rank tracking, which checks your position on a results page that already exists. AI answers are generated fresh for every query, which means there is no static "position" to scrape; the software has to actually query the models itself, repeatedly, and parse unstructured natural-language output into structured metrics.

The minimum feature set looks like this: multi-model prompt execution (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews at a minimum), presence and position detection within the response text, sentiment classification of how your brand is described, competitor comparison on the same prompt set, and citation tracking that shows which URLs the model pulled from to generate the answer.

## Why can't I just use Semrush or Ahrefs for this?

Because their AI tracking features bolt onto an SEO data model that was never designed for generative answers. [Semrush and similar suites typically track AI Overview appearances tied to a specific keyword](https://trackbase.ai/blog/semrush-hubspot-vs-dedicated-ai-visibility-platform), which captures only a narrow slice of how people actually query AI assistants. Real usage is conversational: "what's the best X for Y" rather than a two-word keyword, and most SEO-first tools do not run or score those longer natural-language prompts at all.

They also tend to cover only Google's AI Overviews, leaving ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Gemini, where a large share of commercial research now happens, completely unmeasured. If your dashboard only shows one model, you are looking at a fraction of your actual AI exposure.

## What metrics should the software report?

At minimum: a Visibility Score (how often and how prominently you appear across your tracked prompts), Share of Voice (your presence relative to named competitors on the same prompts), sentiment (how positively or negatively the model describes you), average position when multiple brands appear in one answer, and citation count (how often your own pages are the source the model points to). [These five metrics cover the full picture](https://trackbase.ai/blog/the-5-geo-metrics-that-actually-matter-for-ai-search-visibility); a tool that only reports raw mention counts is giving you a fraction of the signal.

Mentions and citations are not the same thing and software that conflates them will mislead you. A mention is your brand name appearing in the answer text. A citation is the model pointing to one of your URLs as a source. You can be mentioned without being cited, and cited without being named directly. Track both.

## How often does the tracking need to refresh?

Daily, or close to it, because AI answers drift faster than search rankings ever did. Models update continuously, retrieval indexes refresh on their own cadence, and a competitor publishing one well-placed comparison page can shift an answer within days. Software that only re-runs your prompts monthly will consistently tell you about a problem weeks after it started.

This is also why a single manual check in a ChatGPT tab is close to worthless as a tracking method: it tells you about one session, one phrasing, one moment in time, with no way to separate a fluke from a trend.

## Does it need to cover ChatGPT Shopping and commerce surfaces too?

If you sell a physical product, yes. ChatGPT's shopping and product carousel features are a distinct surface from conversational answers, with their own ranking logic around price, tags, and merchant data. A tracking platform aimed at ecommerce or retail brands should monitor carousel position and price positioning separately from standard prompt visibility, because the two behave differently and need different fixes.

## What should you check before choosing a platform?

Confirm model coverage first: does it track the assistants your actual customers use, not just Google's AI Overview. Then check refresh frequency, since a tool that updates weekly cannot catch the kind of rapid drift described above. Check whether it separates mentions from citations, since that distinction drives different actions. Finally, check whether it gives you a prioritized next step for a weak score, rather than just a dashboard of numbers with no path to improvement.
