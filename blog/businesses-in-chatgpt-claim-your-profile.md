# Businesses in ChatGPT: How to Claim Your Business Profile

> Businesses in ChatGPT lets you claim a business profile inside ChatGPT. Learn how claiming works, what Insights misses and a first-week plan.

*By Levi Bouman · Published 2026-11-25 · Updated 2026-11-25 · Original article: https://trackbase.ai/blog/businesses-in-chatgpt-claim-your-profile*

Businesses in ChatGPT is OpenAI's beta program that lets a company claim and manage its own business profile inside ChatGPT. Claiming is free for now, it takes a verification step, and it does not guarantee that ChatGPT recommends you. Treat the profile as one input to [AI visibility](https://trackbase.ai/glossary/ai-visibility), not as the whole strategy. Trackbase has already been approved in the program, so this guide also reflects what we saw first-hand.

## What is Businesses in ChatGPT?

Businesses in ChatGPT is a beta program, reported as launched on November 10, 2026, that gives companies a profile they control inside ChatGPT, managed through a Business Hub. The profile holds your company details, an optional product feed and an optional Business Agent: a customer-facing chat that answers product and policy questions, built from your instructions and feed.

Per the detailed walkthrough by Promptwatch, the Business Agent is currently US-only, limited to one per profile, and must disclose that it is an AI. Checkout and bookings stay on your own website. Availability and rules can change during a beta, so confirm the current terms with OpenAI before you plan around them.

## How to claim your business in ChatGPT

The process has four parts. Based on public documentation and Promptwatch's test, expect this order:

1.  **Open Business Hub** and search for your company. If ChatGPT already lists it, your claim may go to a waitlist. If ChatGPT does not know you yet, you can create a profile.

2.  **Verify ownership** with a business phone number, business email, DNS TXT record, or a Shopify connection.

3.  **Pass the identity check.** Promptwatch reports a government ID check through Persona after approval.

4.  **Finalize the profile before approval.** Promptwatch reports that approved profiles go live automatically and that OpenAI Support cannot remove them. Treat the draft as a publication.

## What to check before the profile goes live

Because a profile goes live on approval, run through this list first:

-   Legal name, website and category match what your site and other listings say.

-   Descriptions are factual and consistent with your homepage and `llms.txt`.

-   Product titles and descriptions follow the feed limits (reported as 150 and 5,000 characters).

-   Availability values in the feed are correct. Out-of-stock items marked as available create bad answers.

-   Your policies (returns, shipping, pricing) are published on pages ChatGPT can read.

## Profile, product feed and Business Agent

| Element | What it does | What it does not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business profile | Gives ChatGPT verified facts about you | Guarantee mentions or rankings |
| Product feed (CSV or JSONL, auto-sync for Shopify) | Makes products available to ChatGPT | Guarantee recommendations |
| Business Agent | Answers product and policy questions | Handle checkout or bookings |

The feed spec reportedly has 48 fields, 9 of them required. A clean feed is a prerequisite, not a ranking lever. ChatGPT still draws on other sources, so reviews, press, directories and your own content keep mattering. ## What the Insights dashboard measures, and what it misses

Business Hub includes Insights with 7-day and 28-day views. Promptwatch reports that "mentions" count the times ChatGPT referenced your website, that data lags at least a day, and that small numbers can be hidden. One early benchmark it cites logged 690 mentions and 24 clicks in 28 days, a 3.5% click rate.

Insights has clear gaps:

-   It does not show **which prompts** triggered a mention.

-   It does not show **competitors** or your share of voice.

-   Brand names without a link are not counted.

-   It covers **ChatGPT only**, not Gemini, Perplexity, Claude or Google AI Overview.

-   ChatGPT stopped sending referrers on October 8, 2026 (per Promptwatch), so referrer-based analytics undercount.

## Does a profile help with ChatGPT Ads?

Promptwatch reports that new profiles were offered ad credit and that a paid "Sponsored Agents" option lets a business agent act as an ad destination, with placements labeled and separate from answers. These offers are early and may change. If you plan to advertise, keep organic and paid measurement separate. ## Your first-week plan

1.  **Day 1:** Audit your facts. Make site, listings and feed say the same thing.

2.  **Day 2:** Claim and verify, then review every field before approval.

3.  **Day 3:** Upload the product feed and check availability values.

4.  **Day 4:** Write down 10-20 prompts customers use, and track them across models. Our guide to [tracking what ChatGPT says about your company](https://trackbase.ai/blog/track-what-chatgpt-says-about-your-company) shows how.

5.  **Day 5:** Record a baseline: visibility, citations, sentiment, competitors.

6.  **Day 7:** Compare against Insights, then fix gaps (missing pages, inconsistent facts).

## FAQ

**Is Businesses in ChatGPT free?**Per current reports it is free during the beta. OpenAI may introduce paid options, so check the official terms.

**Will claiming my profile make ChatGPT recommend my business?**No. A profile gives ChatGPT verified facts, but recommendations still depend on your wider presence: your site, reviews, press and the sources models cite.
