# How AI Models Decide Which Brands to Recommend

> Ask ChatGPT for the best CRM, the best running shoes, the best AI visibility tool. You get a shortlist back. That shortlist is not random, and it is also not the result of any single thing.

*By Levi Bouman · Published 2026-06-20 · Updated 2026-11-11 · Original article: https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-ai-models-decide-which-brands-to-recommend*

AI models decide which brands to recommend by combining three layers: what they learned in training, what they can retrieve live from the web, and how often independent sources talk about your brand. Which layer dominates depends on the prompt, the model and the market, so the same brand can win one answer and be absent from the next.

Ask ChatGPT for the best CRM, the best running shoes or the best AI visibility tool and you get a shortlist back. That shortlist is not random, and it is not the result of any single factor. This article explains each layer, shows how they combine, and ends with a checklist of what you can influence.

## Layer 1: what the model already knows

Every large language model is trained on a snapshot of the public web: Wikipedia, Reddit, review sites, news archives, forums, comparison blogs and millions of marketing pages. If your brand appears often in those sources, and in contexts that look authoritative, the model ties your name to the right category.

That is why brands with strong PR coverage, an active community and a Wikipedia entry tend to win AI shortlists by default. The model has simply seen them more often, in places that matter.

Training data has a cutoff, though. A brand that launched after the last training round is invisible at this layer until the next refresh, so newer companies have to lean harder on the next two layers.

## Layer 2: what the model can look up

Most modern assistants do not rely on memory alone. ChatGPT Search, [Perplexity](https://trackbase.ai/blog/the-11-ai-platforms-every-brand-should-be-tracking-in-2026), Gemini, Google AI Overviews and Google AI Mode run live searches when the question calls for it. This retrieval layer pulls in fresh content, summarises it and folds it into the answer. The sub-searches a model runs behind one answer are called query fan-outs, and they show you which intents the model is really trying to satisfy.

This is good news if you launched recently. A well-structured page that matches the right prompts can appear in AI answers within weeks, even without years of [citations](https://trackbase.ai/blog/mentions-vs-citations-why-both-matter-for-your-ai-visibility) behind it.

The catch is that retrieval favours pages that are easy to extract from. Vague intros, missing headings and walls of text without a clear claim get passed over. AirOps reports that pages with clean structure and schema earn 2.8 times more AI citations than poorly formatted ones ([AirOps](https://www.airops.com/blog/ai-search-metrics)). AirOps sells AI search tooling and does not disclose its sample, so read the number as directional, but it matches what we see: answer-first pages get cited.

## Layer 3: who else is talking about you

This is the layer most brands underinvest in. Models weigh independent mentions more heavily than your own marketing copy. A review on a respected platform carries more signal than a homepage tagline, and an editorial piece carries more than a blog post you wrote yourself.

Five surfaces do most of the work:

-   Reviews on G2, Capterra and TrustRadius

-   Editorial coverage in respected industry publications

-   Wikipedia and Wikidata entries, if your brand qualifies

-   Discussion threads on Reddit, Hacker News and niche forums where buyers actually spend time

-   Comparison pages and listicles maintained by third parties

Not every mention is equal. Models seem to discount obviously promotional content: a balanced comparison from an analyst usually does more than a glowing affiliate review. You can see which third-party pages a model leans on in the [cited sources](https://trackbase.ai/glossary/cited-sources) of your tracked prompts.

## Layer 4: fit with the specific prompt

Even with all three layers on your side, the model still has to decide whether your brand fits the prompt in front of it. A brand can dominate "enterprise CRM" answers and be invisible for "CRM for solo consultants". The fit calculation looks at intent, segment, geography and sometimes price band.

This is also why results differ per model. Each model interprets fit slightly differently and draws from a slightly different source pool, so ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity regularly name different brands for the same question.

## The layers at a glance

| Layer | Where the signal comes from | Speed of change | What you can do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Training knowledge | Public web snapshot at training time | Months to years | Build lasting PR, community and reference-site presence |
| Live retrieval | Indexed pages found at answer time | Days to weeks | Publish extractable, answer-first, well-structured pages |
| Third-party mentions | Reviews, editorial, forums, comparisons | Weeks to months | Earn balanced coverage on the sources models already cite |
| Prompt fit | Match between your positioning and the question | Depends on your content | Cover each segment and use case with its own page |

## Why one answer is never the full picture

AI answers are volatile. AirOps' *2026 State of AI Search* reports that only 30% of brands stay visible from one answer to the next, and just 20% across five consecutive runs. The same research says brands earning both citations and mentions are 40% more likely to resurface across multiple answers than brands with citations alone. Treat those figures as vendor-sourced, but the direction is consistent with what daily tracking shows: a single check tells you very little, a trend tells you a lot.

## What to do with this

If you understand the inputs, you can influence them:

1.  **Earn third-party coverage** in the sources models already trust for your category.

2.  **Structure pages for extraction**: a direct answer first, clear H2 sections, a table or list, and [structured data](https://trackbase.ai/glossary/structured-data).

3.  **Show up in comparison content** for your category, including pages you do not own.

4.  **Cover each segment**, so the fit calculation has something to match.

5.  **Measure daily**, per model, so you know which layer is limiting you.

None of these are quick wins, but they compound. ## FAQ

### Do AI models recommend brands based on paid placement?

For organic answers, no: recommendations come from training knowledge, retrieved sources and independent mentions. Advertising formats exist in some assistants, but they are labelled separately from the organic answer.

### How long does it take to appear in AI answers?

New, well-structured pages can show up through live retrieval within weeks. Shifts in training knowledge and third-party reputation take months, which is why you should start with both tracks at once.

### Why does my brand appear in ChatGPT but not in Gemini?

Each model draws on a different source pool and interprets prompt fit differently. Track visibility per model and look at which sources each one cites, then close the gap there instead of optimising for an average.
