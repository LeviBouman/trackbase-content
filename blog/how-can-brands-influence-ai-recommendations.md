# How Can Brands Influence Recommendations Made by AI Assistants?

> You cannot buy your way into an AI recommendation. Here is how training data, retrieval, source authority and consensus actually shape whether a model recommends your brand.

*By Levi Bouman · Published 2026-10-05 · Original article: https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-can-brands-influence-ai-recommendations*

You cannot bribe ChatGPT the way you can buy a sponsored search result. There is no ad auction, no bid, no guaranteed placement. Yet some brands show up in AI recommendations again and again while others never do. **Influence over AI recommendations comes from structured, verifiable presence across the sources models already trust, not from any form of direct payment or submission.**

That distinction matters because most teams approach this the wrong way. They ask "how do I get listed" when the real question is "why would a model trust this answer enough to repeat it." Those are different problems with different fixes.

## Can brands actually influence what AI assistants recommend?

Yes, but indirectly and probabilistically, not through a single lever you can pull once. Large language models generate recommendations by synthesizing patterns across training data and, for most modern assistants, live retrieval from the web at query time. A brand influences that output by shaping three things: what the model already knows, what it can retrieve right now, and how consistently both of those point in the same direction.

This is fundamentally different from SEO, where a single technical fix (a redirect, a meta tag) can move a ranking within days. AI recommendation influence is cumulative. A model forms a "belief" about your brand from dozens of sources over months, and that belief shifts slowly, in proportion to how much of the web agrees with it.

## What actually shapes an AI recommendation?

Four forces determine whether a model recommends your brand: training data exposure, live retrieval results, source authority, and cross-source consensus. [We cover the mechanics of this in detail elsewhere](https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-ai-models-decide-which-brands-to-recommend), but the practical summary is this: models lean on retrieval-augmented generation to pull current web content for a query, then weigh that content by how authoritative and consistent the sources are.

If ten independent, credible sources describe your product the same way, a model treats that as fact. If your own website is the only place making a claim, the model treats it as marketing and discounts it accordingly.

## How do you map the prompts that matter for your brand?

Start with the handful of queries that actually drive commercial decisions in your category, not an exhaustive keyword list. Pull these from three places: the comparison and "best of" queries your sales team already hears from prospects, the support tickets that reveal what customers are actually confused about, and the SEO queries where you already rank but want to confirm AI parity.

A focused list of 15 to 30 prompts beats a thousand loosely related keywords, because you need to re-run and track each one regularly. **Volume without a repeatable measurement process is just noise.**

## How do you check whether AI is actually recommending you?

A single manual check in ChatGPT tells you almost nothing, because answers vary by model, by session, and by phrasing. You need to run the same prompt set across multiple models (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews at minimum) on a recurring schedule, and record more than just presence.

-   Whether your brand is mentioned at all

-   Whether it is actively recommended, not just referenced in passing

-   Where it ranks relative to competitors in the same answer

-   What sentiment the model attaches to the mention

-   Which sources the model cites to justify the recommendation

That last point, the cited sources, is the single most actionable signal you get. It tells you exactly which pages on the web are shaping the answer, which is where leverage actually exists.

## Which sources should you target to influence the answer?

Follow the citations back to their source and treat the highest-frequency ones as your priority list. If a comparison site, a review platform, or an industry publication keeps showing up as a cited source across your target prompts, that is a page worth influencing directly: through a correction request, a contributed update, a partnership, or simply making sure your own data (pricing, features, positioning) is accurate and current wherever that source pulls it from.

This is slower and less controllable than paid placement, but it is also the only lever that compounds. A single well-placed correction on a frequently-cited comparison page can shift how a model describes you across every query that touches that source.

## Does your own content still matter?

Yes, but as the foundation, not the whole strategy. Your own site needs to say clearly and specifically what you do, for whom, and how you differ from alternatives, structured so a model can extract it cleanly (clear headings, direct answers, no vague marketing language). [The owned-side playbook covers this in more depth](https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-to-improve-your-ai-visibility-a-practical-playbook), but the short version is that owned content sets the baseline claim; third-party sources are what validate it.

Models explicitly discount claims that appear only on a brand's own domain. The same claim repeated by an independent source carries materially more weight.

## How long before you see a change?

Expect weeks to months, not days. Because models retrain periodically and retrieval indexes refresh on their own schedules, a correction made today might not show up in an answer for several weeks. This is why a one-time audit is close to useless: you need a baseline, a change, and then a re-measurement cycle to know if anything actually moved.

Track the same prompt set before and after any content or outreach change, isolate one variable at a time where possible, and give it at least two to four weeks before drawing conclusions. Anything faster is reading noise as signal.
