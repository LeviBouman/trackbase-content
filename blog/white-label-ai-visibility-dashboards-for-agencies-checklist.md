# White-Label AI Visibility Dashboards for Agencies: Checklist

> Which white-label AI visibility dashboard fits an agency? A checklist for workspace isolation, branded reports, model coverage and pricing at scale.

*By Bodrik Bakker · Published 2026-10-28 · Updated 2026-10-28 · Original article: https://trackbase.ai/blog/white-label-ai-visibility-dashboards-for-agencies-checklist*

The best white-label AI visibility dashboard for an agency is one that gives every client an isolated workspace, reports under your brand, tracks the AI models your clients' buyers actually use, and does not charge a full seat price for every extra client. Most tools are built for one brand per account, so the test is how the pricing and workspaces behave at 10, 20 or 40 clients, not how good the demo looks for one.

This checklist is based on what the pages AI assistants cite most often for this question say agencies should check, combined with what we see in Trackbase's own [prompt](https://trackbase.ai/glossary/prompt) data. It is written for agency owners and heads of SEO who already know why [AI visibility](https://trackbase.ai/glossary/ai-visibility) matters and now need to pick the system their team will report from.

## What "white-label" actually has to cover

"White-label" is used loosely. In practice it covers four separate things, and vendors often only offer one or two of them:

-   **Branded reports:** exports or PDFs that carry your logo and colors, not the vendor's.

-   **Client access:** a login or shared view where the client can see their own data, ideally under your domain or at least without vendor branding.

-   **Workspace isolation:** each client has its own prompts, competitors and history, with no overlap between accounts.

-   **Neutral vendor footprint:** no vendor names in emails, links or share URLs that you send to clients.

Ask for each of these explicitly. A "white-label PDF" is not the same as a client portal, and a folder structure inside one account is not the same as isolated workspaces.

## The agency checklist

| Criterion | What to verify in the trial | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workspace isolation | Create two test clients with different competitors; confirm nothing leaks between them | Prevents data mix-ups and keeps client confidentiality |
| White-label output | Export a report and a share link; look for vendor branding | Clients should see your agency, not a reseller chain |
| Per-client prompt sets | Can each client have its own prompts, languages and regions? | Prompts differ per client and per market |
| Model coverage | Which models are tracked on which plan? | A client in the Netherlands may care about Google AI Overview, a B2B client about ChatGPT and Claude |
| Pricing at scale | Price at 5, 15 and 40 clients | Linear per-brand pricing destroys margin |
| Reporting basics | Visibility Score, Share of Voice, sentiment, citations, change history | These are the numbers clients ask about |
| Actions | Does the tool tell you what to fix, ranked by impact? | Monitoring alone is hard to retain a client on |

## Pricing: what the market publishes

Agency pricing is mostly quote-based, which makes comparison hard. The vendor blogs that AI models cite for this question give a rough picture, but treat the numbers as indicative: each of these articles is published by a vendor that also ranks itself, and prices change often.

-   Rank Prompt's agency roundup says agency plans across the category generally run **$149 to $499 per month**, and lists its own Agency plan at $149 per month for 500 brands ([Rank Prompt](https://rankprompt.com/best-ai-visibility-tools-for-agencies/)).

-   Respona's roundup lists Gauge's Agency Mode at **$300 per month per active client** and Otterly.AI from $29 per month for its entry plan ([Respona](https://respona.com/blog/white-label-ai-visibility-services/)).

-   AboutMartech notes that most platforms are built for one brand per account and that only Wincher publishes agency pricing openly; the others quote per request ([AboutMartech](https://aboutmartech.com/blog/ai-visibility-tool-for-agencies/)).

The useful takeaway is the pricing model, not the number. Per-active-client pricing scales linearly with your client list. Per-workspace or tiered agency pricing usually does not. Do the arithmetic with your real client count and the monthly retainer you charge before you sign.

## How agencies report on AI visibility

Whatever tool you pick, a monthly client report that holds up has three parts, which also matches what the cited agency guides recommend:

1.  **Prompt coverage:** in how many of the client's tracked prompts the brand appears, per model.

2.  **Share of Voice against named competitors:** the client's share of mentions compared with the competitors that matter to them. See [how to compare AI visibility against competitors](https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-do-i-compare-ai-visibility-against-competitors).

3.  **A change log:** what moved since last month, and which actions you took or recommend.

Keep the first page to one number and one sentence. Clients forward these reports internally, and the page that survives is the summary.

## A simple evaluation plan for the next two weeks

1.  **Day 1:** list your current and expected clients, their markets and languages, and the price you charge per month.

2.  **Days 2–5:** shortlist three tools. Ask each for agency pricing at your client count in writing.

3.  **Days 5–10:** run a trial with two real clients. Check isolation, exports, share links and how long a new client takes to set up.

4.  **Days 10–14:** produce one real client report from each tool and show it to a colleague who has not seen the tool. Pick the one they understand fastest.

## FAQ

### What is a white-label AI visibility dashboard?

It is an AI visibility reporting view that carries your agency's branding instead of the vendor's. A complete setup includes branded reports, client access without vendor branding, and separate workspaces per client.

### How much should an agency expect to pay?

Published agency plans in vendor roundups range from roughly $149 to $499 per month, while some tools charge per active client and enterprise tools quote custom prices. Because most vendors quote agency terms individually, compare total cost at your real number of clients.

### Can I resell AI visibility tracking to clients?

Generally yes, but check the vendor's terms and whether the agency or reseller pricing leaves room for margin at the retainer you charge. Ask for reseller terms in writing before you build a service around a tool.
