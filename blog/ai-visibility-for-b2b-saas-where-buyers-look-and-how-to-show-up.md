# AI Visibility for B2B SaaS: Where Buyers Look and How to Show Up

> The modern software buying journey often starts in a chat window, not a search bar. A buyer types “best [category] tool for [use case]” into ChatGPT and gets a shortlist back. Your brand is on that list, or it is not, and most of the evaluation happens before anyone visits your homepage.

*By Bodrik Bakker · Published 2026-06-29 · Original article: https://trackbase.ai/blog/ai-visibility-for-b2b-saas-where-buyers-look-and-how-to-show-up*

The modern software buying journey often starts in a chat window, not a search bar. A buyer types “best \[category\] tool for \[use case\]” into ChatGPT and gets a shortlist back. Your brand is on that list, or it is not, and most of the evaluation happens before anyone visits your homepage.

**For B2B SaaS, the AI shortlist is the new top of the funnel.**

## Why SaaS is especially exposed

SaaS buying is high-consideration and comparison-heavy. Buyers ask for alternatives, weigh options against each other, and read what other people say before they commit. Those are exactly the questions [AI answers](https://trackbase.ai/blog/monitor-changes-in-ai-answers-over-time) with a confident, ranked shortlist. Combine that with low switching friction and you get a category where the shortlist stage decides most of the outcome.

## Where SaaS buyers actually look

It helps to think in prompt types, because each one is a separate visibility battle:

-   **Category** [**prompts**](https://trackbase.ai/features/prompt-tracking): “best CRM for startups”, “top project management tools”.

-   **Alternatives prompts**: “alternatives to \[competitor\]”, often from buyers already shopping.

-   **Comparison prompts**: “\[you\] vs \[competitor\]”, the highest-intent of the set.

-   **Use-case prompts**: “tool for \[specific job\]”, where fit beats brand size.

You can dominate one of these and be invisible in another. A brand that owns “enterprise CRM” can be absent from “CRM for solo consultants” entirely.

## The sources that move SaaS shortlists

[AI models](https://trackbase.ai/blog/the-11-ai-platforms-every-brand-should-be-tracking-in-2026) lean hardest on independent signals, and for SaaS a few sources do most of the lifting. This is the same pattern behind [how AI decides which brands to recommend](https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-ai-models-decide-which-brands-to-recommend), weighted for software buyers:

-   Review platforms: G2, Capterra and TrustRadius carry unusual weight in software categories.

-   Reddit and niche communities where buyers ask peers what they actually use.

-   Third-party comparison pages and “best of” listicles for your category.

-   Your own docs and comparison pages, but only if they are structured so a model can extract clean answers from them.

## Where to start

You cannot fix everything at once, so sequence it:

-   Pick your highest-intent prompts first: your category term and your top competitor comparisons.

-   Baseline where you appear across the major platforms today. You cannot improve what you have not measured.

-   Prioritise the sources and pages that feed those specific prompts.

-   Track weekly. SaaS categories and AI answers both move fast, so a quarterly check is already out of date.

The brands that win the SaaS shortlist are not always the biggest. They are the ones showing up consistently in the sources AI trusts, structured for the way models read, and measured often enough to course-correct.
