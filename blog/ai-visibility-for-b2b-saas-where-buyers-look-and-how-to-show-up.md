# AI Visibility for B2B SaaS: Where Buyers Look and How to Show Up

> The modern software buying journey often starts in a chat window, not a search bar. A buyer types “best [category] tool for [use case]” into ChatGPT and gets a shortlist back. Your brand is on that list, or it is not, and most of the evaluation happens before anyone visits your homepage.

*By Bodrik Bakker · Published 2026-06-29 · Updated 2026-11-20 · Original article: https://trackbase.ai/blog/ai-visibility-for-b2b-saas-where-buyers-look-and-how-to-show-up*

For B2B SaaS, the AI shortlist is the new top of the funnel. A buyer types "best \[category\] tool for \[use case\]" into ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity and gets a ranked shortlist back; your brand is on it or it is not, and much of the evaluation happens before anyone visits your homepage. To show up, you need to know which prompts buyers use, which sources the models trust, and measure both often.

## Why SaaS is especially exposed

SaaS buying is high-consideration and comparison-heavy. Buyers ask for alternatives, weigh options against each other and read what peers say before they commit. Those are exactly the questions [AI answers](https://trackbase.ai/blog/monitor-changes-in-ai-answers-over-time) with a confident, ranked list. Add low switching friction and you get a category where the shortlist stage decides most of the outcome.

The buyer data points the same way. A TrustRadius 2025 study of 2,548 respondents, summarised in [Slate's AI share of voice roundup](https://slatehq.com/blog/best-ai-share-of-voice-tools), found that 72% of B2B tech buyers encountered Google AI Overviews while researching software, and 90% of those clicked at least one cited source. Only 7% used LLMs such as ChatGPT directly for buying research, so AI search is already shaping shortlists even where chat adoption is still modest.

## Where SaaS buyers actually look

Think in prompt types, because each is a separate visibility battle:

-   **Category** [**prompts**](https://trackbase.ai/features/prompt-tracking)**:** "best CRM for startups", "top project management tools".

-   **Alternatives prompts:** "alternatives to \[competitor\]", often from buyers already shopping.

-   **Comparison prompts:** "\[you\] vs \[competitor\]", the highest-intent set.

-   **Use-case prompts:** "tool for \[specific job\]", where fit beats brand size.

-   **Integration prompts:** "CRM that integrates with \[tool\]", where documentation quality matters.

You can dominate one type and be invisible in another. A brand that owns "enterprise CRM" can be absent from "CRM for solo consultants" entirely.

## The sources that move SaaS shortlists

[AI models](https://trackbase.ai/blog/the-11-ai-platforms-every-brand-should-be-tracking-in-2026) lean hardest on independent signals. This is the pattern behind [how AI decides which brands to recommend](https://trackbase.ai/blog/how-ai-models-decide-which-brands-to-recommend), weighted for software:

-   Review platforms: G2, Capterra and TrustRadius carry unusual weight in software categories.

-   Reddit and niche communities where buyers ask peers what they use.

-   Third-party comparison pages and "best of" lists for your category.

-   Your own docs and comparison pages, but only if they are structured so a model can extract clean answers.

Many of the most-cited pages in Trackbase source data start with a direct answer, use tables and lists, and name the entities they compare. That matches the Princeton GEO research (arXiv 2311.09735), which links extractable structure such as definitions, lists, tables and attributed sources to higher citation probability.

## A simple prompt map for a SaaS team

| Funnel stage | Example prompt | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awareness | "What is \[category\]?" | Are you cited as a definition source? |
| Consideration | "Best \[category\] for \[use case\]" | Are you on the shortlist, and at which position? |
| Evaluation | "\[You\] vs \[competitor\]" | Is the comparison fair and accurate? |
| Decision | "\[You\] pricing" | Does the model quote current prices? |

Run the same table every week. Position and sentiment matter as much as presence: being listed fifth with a lukewarm description is not the same as being recommended first.

## Mistakes SaaS teams make

-   **Tracking only the brand name.** Buyers rarely type your name; they type the problem. Track category and use-case prompts, not just branded ones.

-   **Treating review sites as an afterthought.** If your G2 or Capterra profile is thin or outdated, models inherit that picture.

-   **Publishing comparison pages with no structure.** A wall of marketing copy is hard to quote. A table with clear criteria is easy to cite.

-   **Measuring once.** One audit gives a baseline, not a trend. Without repeated runs you cannot tell whether a change worked.

## FAQ

**How is AI visibility different from SEO for a SaaS company?**SEO targets ranked links; AI visibility targets whether a model names and recommends you inside an answer. The two overlap, but a page can rank well and still be absent from AI shortlists. See [AI visibility vs SEO](https://trackbase.ai/blog/ai-visibility-vs-seo).

**Which prompts should a B2B SaaS team track first?**Start with your category prompt and your top three competitor comparison prompts, then add alternatives and use-case prompts. Keep the set fixed so trends are meaningful.

**How often should we measure?**Daily runs are ideal because AI answers vary between sessions; weekly review of the trend is the minimum. A quarterly check is too slow for a fast-moving software category.
