# Compare Platforms That Track Changes in AI Answers Over Time

> How to compare AI visibility platforms on run frequency, stored responses, history and actions, with a side-by-side look at Trackbase, Peec AI, Otterly and Profound.

*By Bodrik Bakker · Published 2026-11-18 · Updated 2026-11-18 · Original article: https://trackbase.ai/blog/compare-platforms-that-track-changes-in-ai-answers-over-time*

The best platform for tracking changes in AI answers is the one that runs your prompts every day, stores the full response for every model and keeps that history long enough to compare month against month. Most tools in this category check the basics; they differ on run frequency, how much history they keep, and whether a change leads to an action. Use the six criteria below to compare Trackbase, Otterly, Peec AI, Profound and the SEO suites on equal terms.

## Why "tracking over time" is the hard part of AI visibility

A single snapshot of an AI answer tells you almost nothing. Models sample their output, retrieval sources rotate and competitors publish new pages, so the same [prompt](https://trackbase.ai/glossary/prompt) can return a different shortlist tomorrow. What you need is a time series: the same prompt, the same model, run on a fixed schedule, with the answer stored. Our guide on [how to monitor changes in AI answers about your company](https://trackbase.ai/blog/monitor-changes-in-ai-answers-over-time) explains the routine; this article is about choosing the platform that makes that routine possible.

The pages AI models cite most for this question share a pattern. In our Trackbase data for the last 30 days, the prompt "Compare platforms that track changes in AI answers and brand mentions over time" produced 2,165 citations across 870 sources. The most-cited were the vendor homepages of Otterly (43), Profound (39) and Peec AI (38), followed by two comparison articles from Search Atlas (28) and Surmado (27). Models lean on vendor pages for facts and on comparison articles for structure, so a good comparison needs both.

## The six criteria that separate platforms

Search Atlas scored 18 platforms against a ten-field rubric in October 2026, and history, citations, prompt controls and pricing were all on it. For change tracking specifically, six of those fields matter most:

| Criterion | What to ask | Why it matters for change tracking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Run frequency | Daily, weekly or on demand? | Weekly snapshots blur the moment a shift happened |
| Response storage | Full answer text or only a score? | Only stored answers explain why a score moved |
| History length | How long is data kept and exportable? | Year-over-year and pre/post model-update comparisons |
| Model coverage | Which engines, per plan? | Changes are model-specific; one engine hides the rest |
| Source tracking | Are cited URLs recorded per run? | Most changes trace back to a source entering or leaving the answer |
| Next step | Does a change turn into an action? | Detection without action is a report nobody reads |

Search Atlas notes that weekly sampling is a common baseline for core prompts and that daily suits launches and campaigns. We would go one step further: for change detection, daily is the default, because you want three or more consecutive data points before you call something a trend rather than noise.

## A one-week test before you commit

Search Atlas suggests running five prompts yourself in the same engines and comparing them with the platform's records. We would add four checks specific to change tracking:

1.  **Start two trials in parallel** and load the same five prompts into each.

2.  **After seven days, open the response history** and check that each day's full answer and [cited sources](https://trackbase.ai/glossary/cited-sources) are stored, not just a number.

3.  **Compare day-to-day variation.** If a tool shows a flat line while your manual checks vary, it is probably smoothing the data.

4.  **Ask how a change surfaces.** Is there an alert, a filter for biggest movers, or a recommended action?

## FAQ

### How often should a platform run my prompts to catch changes?

Daily. Weekly runs are fine for stable core prompts, but they hide when a change started, which is the information you need to find the cause. Daily data also lets you require three consecutive days before reacting, which filters out sampling noise.

### Do I need to track every AI model to see changes?

No, but you should track the models your buyers use. Changes are model-specific, so a single-engine tool can miss a drop elsewhere. If budget is tight, start with ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity and add more as the prompt set grows.

### Is an SEO suite enough for tracking AI answer changes?

It can be a start if you already pay for one, but dedicated tools usually give more control over prompts, per-model history and cited sources. Many teams use both for now: the suite for rankings, a dedicated platform for the daily AI answer series.
