# Affordable AI Visibility Tools for Dutch Startups (2026)

> What do AI visibility tools really cost? Compare entry prices from $20 to €199 a month and pick the plan that fits a Dutch startup's prompts, models and budget.

*By Bodrik Bakker · Published 2026-10-22 · Updated 2026-10-22 · Original article: https://trackbase.ai/blog/affordable-ai-visibility-tools-for-dutch-startups*

For a Dutch startup, the affordable end of [AI visibility](https://trackbase.ai/glossary/ai-visibility) tracking runs from roughly $20 to €50 a month, and the right pick depends on how many prompts and AI models you need to watch. Budget trackers such as Rankscale and Otterly start around $20 to $29 a month, Trackbase starts at €49 a month with a 14-day free trial, and suite add-ons from Semrush or Ahrefs cost $99 to $199 a month on top of a plan you already pay for. Before you pay for any of them, run a free check to see whether AI models mention your startup at all.

## What should "affordable" mean for a startup?

A low sticker price is only half the answer. What matters is the price per useful data point: how many prompts you can track, on how many AI models, how often, and whether the tool tells you what to do next. A $20 plan that tracks a handful of prompts occasionally and a €49 plan that tracks 10 prompts daily are not the same product.

Four numbers decide whether a plan fits an early-stage team:

-   **Prompts tracked.** Ten to twenty well-chosen buyer questions are enough to start. You are measuring a category, not the whole internet.

-   **AI models covered.** ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity cover most B2B discovery. Claude and [Google AI Overviews](https://trackbase.ai/glossary/google-ai-overviews) matter if your buyers are technical or search-driven.

-   **Refresh rate.** AI answers shift constantly, so daily runs beat weekly snapshots.

-   **Competitors tracked.** Without at least three rivals, a score is a number with no context.

## Which option fits which kind of startup?

Match the tool to the job, not to the longest feature list.

-   **Pre-product-market-fit, tiny budget.** Use a free check first, then a $20 to $29 tracker for a handful of prompts. You mostly need to learn whether you appear at all.

-   **Seed-stage B2B SaaS with a content engine.** You need competitors, sentiment and [cited sources](https://trackbase.ai/glossary/cited-sources), not only counts. A plan with prioritized actions saves analyst time you do not have.

-   **Already on Semrush or Ahrefs.** The add-on is the cheapest route to a baseline, as long as you accept that it lives inside a suite.

-   **Selling to EU buyers.** Where the data is stored and processed becomes a procurement question quickly. Trackbase is a Dutch company with EU-hosted, GDPR-compliant data, which removes one line from your security questionnaire.

## What changes when you are a Dutch startup specifically?

Three things make the Netherlands different from a generic US buyer guide.

**Euro pricing and VAT.** Most roundups quote dollars. Trackbase's published prices are in euros and exclude VAT, so you can budget without exchange-rate drift. Check whether a USD-priced tool invoices in euros and how it handles Dutch VAT before you commit.

**Dutch-language prompts.** Your buyers may ask in Dutch ("welke tool meet AI-zichtbaarheid voor een klein bedrijf?") or in English. Models answer differently in each language and cite different sources. Ask any vendor whether you can track prompts in both, and track your real buyer phrasing rather than translated keywords.

**GDPR and data location.** If you handle client data or sell into regulated sectors, EU hosting is a practical advantage, not a nice-to-have.

## How do you avoid wasting the first month?

A cheap plan wasted on bad prompts is still wasted. Follow this sequence:

1.  Run the [free AI visibility checker](https://trackbase.ai/free-ai-visibility-checker) to learn whether models name your brand today.

2.  Write 10 to 20 prompts the way buyers ask them: category questions ("best X for Y"), comparison questions, and problem questions.

3.  Pick three direct competitors and track them from day one.

4.  Review results weekly, not daily. Daily collection, weekly decisions.

5.  Turn the gaps into work: pages that answer the [prompt](https://trackbase.ai/glossary/prompt), sources that cite your competitors, and technical fixes.

Measuring is only worth paying for if it changes what you publish. Our guide on [the five GEO metrics that matter](https://trackbase.ai/blog/the-5-geo-metrics-that-actually-matter-for-ai-search-visibility) explains which numbers deserve your attention first.
